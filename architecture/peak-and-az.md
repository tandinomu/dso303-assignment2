# How the design handles the exam peak and the loss of one AZ

**Exam peak.** 3,000 students starting within 5 minutes means about 10 exam starts per second. While they write, autosave every 30 s adds about 100 small writes per second. CloudFront serves static files from the edge, so only API calls reach the VPC. A scheduled scaling action raises the Fargate service to 12 tasks (6 per AZ) 30 minutes before each exam, so capacity is ready when students arrive. Target-tracking scaling on CPU and ALB request count adds more tasks if needed. Autosaves go to DynamoDB in on-demand mode, which absorbs bursts without capacity planning. Aurora only serves exam reads, and RDS Proxy pools those connections. Auto-marking and results emails go through SQS, so the queue buffers slow work and it never blocks a student's request.

**Loss of one AZ.** Every tier runs in both AZs. When an AZ fails, its targets fail the ALB health checks, and the ALB sends all traffic to the healthy AZ within seconds. Each AZ can carry the whole peak alone (static stability), and ECS also starts replacement tasks in the surviving AZ. If the Aurora writer was in the lost AZ, Aurora promotes the reader in the other AZ, usually in under 2 minutes. RDS Proxy holds client connections during the switch. Each AZ has its own NAT Gateway, so outbound traffic does not depend on the failed AZ. S3, DynamoDB, SQS, Lambda, Cognito and CloudFront are regional or global services, so they keep working.

**Targets.** Autosave every 30 s, plus point-in-time recovery on DynamoDB and Aurora, keeps answer loss well under the 5-minute RPO. Multi-AZ failover takes minutes, and a full restore from AWS Backup fits inside the 1-hour RTO. TLS and KMS encryption protect data in transit and at rest. Backups are kept for 5 years.
